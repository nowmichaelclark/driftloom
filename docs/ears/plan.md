# Ears plan: every album on the list

Ears 2 (Opus 5.5), 2026-10-15; updated in Ears 3 (tools and costs) and Ears 5
(costs, pile 1 done). How the ears sessions get a few sounds,
techniques and ideas from every album on Mikey's list. Read
`docs/ears/sessions.md` first. Results go in this folder, in the repo:
`docs/ears/batch-N.md` (verdicts) and `docs/ears/cards-*.md` (album cards).
**Everything an ears session writes lives in the repo (Mikey, 2026-10-17);
see the rule at the top of `docs/ears/sessions.md`.**

## What Mikey asked for (2026-10-15)

- Take something from **every** album on the list: sounds and instruments
  (several from some albums), techniques, compositional ideas and, where
  possible, more music theory.
- Now and then a listener should think "this reminds me of that album."
- Keep everything Driftloom can do today; the albums widen it.
- Wholesome, never depressing. Coffee-shop pleasant is no longer the bar
  for every loop (a vibe can ask for that). Harsh albums are fair game:
  take what's wholesome from them.
- Ears listen, measure and propose. The brain and hands build. Ears only
  build sketches for listening and testing (offline clips), never the app
  or the core.

## Mikey's answers (2026-10-15)

- **No favorites:** every album on the list is 9/10 or 10/10 all the way
  through. Card sessions choose the tracks (below).
- **The Aphex range** means everything released between *SAW 85-92* and
  *Drukqs*, aliases included (list below).
- **Voices: never real words.** Vowels, hums and choirs only.
- **No quotes at all**, public domain included (no "Ode to Joy").
- **Vibes:** new sounds join the existing vibes, by weight. New vibes and
  new profiles (characters) are welcome, with words in the spirit of
  today's (`docs/VIBES.md`, `docs/MOODS.md`). Harsher sounds sit behind
  particular vibes: one shouldn't turn up at random under "happy" and
  "peaceful". It's all weights, never a ban: a vibe can now and then get
  a brief or subtle touch of a sound that isn't its own, and no sound is
  kept away from a vibe "just because". Some vibes are wide open:
  "zany" can take almost anything.
- **Order and workload:** no preference; he'll listen to as much as it
  takes. Ears work at the pace that makes sense.

## The album card

One card per album, about a screen long:

- **The hook:** the one thing that makes a listener say "that's them"
  (BoC: detuned, wobbling pads over a dusty 90 bpm break).
- **Sounds (2–4):** each with a reference track and timestamp, what it is
  in synth terms (source, partials, attack and decay, filter, movement,
  width), its measured fingerprint, the building blocks it would need,
  and a yardstick.
- **Techniques (1–3):** production and arrangement moves (a filter arc,
  a floor drop, tape speed changes, chopped micro-loops, a build), each
  with where it happens.
- **Theory (1–2):** harmony, rhythm, melody or form, written as something
  the generator could draw (a spread, not a rule).
- **Space:** where the record puts things left to right, by band and by
  part. Feeds the space recipes.
- **Vibes:** for each sound and technique, the vibes that lean toward it
  (×1.5 to ×3), the ones that lean away (×0.2 to ×0.5, never 0), and any
  new vibe or profile it suggests. Harsh sounds lean toward their own
  vibes and stay rare elsewhere.
- **Wholesome take:** what to take, what to leave.
- **For later:** everything else worth taking someday, each with track,
  timestamp and a line on what it is and why it waits (too close to
  another sound, too costly now, needs a building block that doesn't
  exist yet). So nobody has to dig through the album again.

## How one album gives several sounds and ideas

1. **Choose the tracks.** Every track gets the cheap mix-level measures
   (`analyze.py`). From those, pick for the deep pass the track most
   typical of the album and the two or three most unlike the rest (by
   bands, tails, pulse, width, chord rate); for an EP, every track. The
   others still feed "For later" from their mix-level numbers and a
   spectrogram look.
2. **Split each track.** Harmonic against percussive, then stems (drums,
   bass, the rest, voice) where the separator installs; band splits where
   it doesn't.
3. **Cut the stems into events and group them by sound.** Each group of
   similar notes is an instrument candidate. For each: register, attack,
   decay, brightness over the note, how harmonic its partials are (string
   or reed vs. bell or metal), vibrato and detune, level against the mix,
   and where it sits left to right.
4. **Follow each track through time.** Section boundaries; when each stem
   enters and leaves; filter and brightness sweeps; echo times; pumping
   locked to the kick; tape wobble and speed changes; movement across
   the stereo field. These are the techniques.
5. **Read the notes.** Key and mode, chord vocabulary, how long chords
   last, what the bass does (pedal, ostinato, walking, slides), the lead's
   intervals and contour, meter, swing, polyrhythm, phrase and section
   lengths, how much repeats. These are the theory.
6. **Write the card**, numbers and timestamps attached. Opus sessions turn
   cards into candidates and clips.

**The tools now exist (Ears 3, PR #221):** `tools/ears/album.py` does steps
1–5, the space step (width by band and stem) and drafts step 6; judgment
lines are marked **[JUDGMENT]**. Two test cards, *Geogaddi* and *Galaxy
Garden*, are in `docs/ears/cards-test.md`. What it does well: sounds
grouped from stems, sections, stem entries, sweeps, wobble, floor drops,
key and mode, chord changes, bass behavior, meter and swing, and space.
What it does not: the top line is only reliable when it sits clear of the
accompaniment; tempo can be half or double; bar length needs an accent;
chord names on dense music are a guess and changes faster than 0.5 s are
undercounted; the vibes, the hook and why something waits are always a
person's call. Where each stops being reliable: `docs/EARS.md`.

**Ears 5 added (PR #223 to merge):** a spot-check script,
`tools/ears/check_stems.py`, to run on each deep-pass track before its card
is written. Pile 1 showed the card tools need it: the separator often puts
the sub in the drums stem (so a stems-only floor drop count is too small and
a near-empty bass stem voids the bass numbers), a track with empty drums and
bass stems has no beat (its tempo, swing and on-beat numbers mean nothing),
the meter tool reports half-time, and a note group at a third of the strongest
spectral line is a subharmonic misread. The list is in `docs/EARS.md`.

**What a pile costs.** Measured on five albums (Ears 5): one album is about
**40 minutes** of machine time on 2 cores (28 to 43), mostly Demucs, which
runs while the session waits (Drive download time is extra). A good estimate
is **1.6 seconds per second of deep-pass audio, plus 25 seconds for every
track in the album** (picking looks at all of them). Demucs ran 1.0 to 1.1
times the audio length; measuring about 3 minutes a track. Five albums took
11,582 s (3.2 hours). The old figure (30 minutes a four-track album) was low
by a quarter. Pile 2 (14 records, mostly EPs where every track gets the deep
pass) now looks like **7 to 8 hours**: 13 EPs of 3 to 6 tracks at 4 to 6
minutes each, plus *...I Care Because You Do* (12 tracks, so 4 get the deep
pass). That is an estimate; Ears 6 should time its first EP and correct it.
Piles 10 to 12 (10 to 12 albums each) are likewise about 7 hours each. Split
pile 2 and piles 10 to 12 into two sessions each (the later labels shift
by four). The other piles of 6 to 8 albums are 4 to 5 hours and fit one
session.

## Theory threads across the list

Ideas that recur across several records, each something the generator
could draw from a spread:

- **Loops of different lengths** (Eno's tape loops, Reich, Konrad
  Sprenger?): layers repeat at 3, 4, 5 or 7 bars, so the whole never quite
  repeats. A direct answer to "loops circle" (travel 0.96 against 1.34).
- **One motif, changing harmony** (*Promises*, Basic Channel): the tune
  stays; the chords under it move slowly.
- **Theme and variations** (Beethoven 9): a theme returns changed:
  instrument, register, rhythm, harmony.
- **Modal vamps** (*Bitches Brew*, krautrock): one chord, one mode; it
  develops by density and timbre.
- **Odd meters and polymeter** (Jaga Jazzist, Autechre, Boards of Canada).
- **Pedal points and drones** (Steve Roach, Robert Ashley, Cluster).
- **Long builds** (Godspeed, Blanck Mass, Beethoven): a five- to ten-minute
  arc of layers, next to the approved filter arc.
- **Scales beyond major and minor** (*Kankyō Ongaku*; check Pauline Anna Strom):
  pentatonic modes, pelog and slendro approximations.
- **Swing as a dial** (*Animal Crossing*, *Windowlicker*, Boards of
  Canada).
- **Speed changes** (*Neu! 2*): a whole loop shifts tempo and pitch
  together.
- **Speech-shaped melody** (Robert Ashley): phrases follow spoken rhythm.
- **Time of day** (*Animal Crossing*'s hourly themes): ties into the games
  roadmap.
- **Smooth voice leading** (Beethoven, fusion): common tones held between
  chords.
